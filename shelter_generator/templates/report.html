GENERAL NOTE
BS EN 12811-1, 4.2.1.2 / 10.3.2.2 Scaffold tube material has a minimum nominal yield strength of 235 N/mm² (BS EN 12811-1, 4.2.1.2). A partial safety factor γM = 1.1 (BS EN 12811-1, 10.3.2.2) has been applied, giving a design resistance of 213.6 N/mm² used in member capacity checks. This design calculation was carried out in accordance with the limit states design method stipulated in EN 1993-1-1:2005 (Eurocode 3 - Design of Steel Structures).
Material Properties

48.3 mm outside diameter x 3.6 mm thick steel tube was defined by STAAD.Pro Connect Edition V22 section tables (British) and assigned to all scaffold members as section PIP483.6.

Modelling Philosophy
BS EN 12811-1, 10.2.3.2 / 10.2.3.3 A 3-D model was analysed using STAAD.Pro CONNECT Edition (V22) in compliance with EN 1993-1-1:2005. Base supports are modelled as pinned with lateral spring stiffness representing frictional restraint (KFX/KFZ = {{ structural.frictional_resistance.kfx }} kN). Base supports are modelled as elastic spring supports, restrained laterally in both horizontal directions with rotational degrees of freedom released, representing flexible ground contact conditions at each standard foot.

APPENDIX E — COUNTERWEIGHT DESIGN CALCULATION (A–Z)
Design approach: An initial counterweight estimate of 1 kN/m² applied uniformly over the counterweight zone was used for the first STAAD analysis run. This is the standard iterative procedure — a non-trivial initial load is necessary to obtain meaningful support reactions, from which the actual counterweight demand is then derived by moment equilibrium (see F.3). The member loads shown in F.5 represent the final design values applied in the current STAAD model.
F.1 — Design Basis
EN 1990:2002 EQU The scaffold is a cantilever arrangement. Imposed loads on the overhang create an overturning moment about the support edge. Stability is verified at the Equilibrium (EQU) limit state per EN 1990:2002 — the appropriate limit state where loss of static equilibrium governs over material failure. The EQU partial factors embedded in the STAAD ULS combinations are:
• Destabilising permanent action (DL): γdst = 1.5
• Destabilising variable action (LL, WL): γdst = 1.5
• Stabilising permanent action (CW): γstb = {{ counterweight.governing_cw_factor }}

The check is satisfied when the stabilising moment from the counterweight — at its reduced factored value — equals or exceeds the overturning moment from all destabilising actions at their amplified values. This is assessed through the STAAD ULS combination results.
F.2 — Geometry: Pivot Line, Counterweight Zone and Lever Arm
STAAD model geometry Pivot line: The row of base support nodes at the edge of the supporting structure about which overturning is assessed — nodes {{ counterweight.pivot_nodes | join(', ') }}, located at {{ counterweight.pivot_axis | upper }} = {{ counterweight.pivot_normal_m }} m.

Counterweight zone: The plan polygon on the backspan platform designated for kentledge placement, bounded by nodes {{ counterweight.zone_nodes | join(', ') }} at elevation y = {{ counterweight.zone_elevation_m }} m.
Zone plan area:  ACW = {{ counterweight.zone_area_m2 }} m²

Zone centroid (geometric centre of the polygon) at {{ counterweight.pivot_axis | upper }} = {{ counterweight.cw_centroid_normal_m }} m.

Lever arm (perpendicular distance from CW centroid to pivot line):
LCW = |{{ counterweight.cw_centroid_normal_m }}  − {{ counterweight.pivot_normal_m }}| = {{ counterweight.cw_lever_m }} m
F.3 — Overturning Moment and Minimum Required Counterweight
Moment balance method Vertical base support reactions (FY) are read from the STAAD output for every ULS combination across all base nodes. STAAD sign convention: positive FY = upward push from ground (compression); negative FY = structure lifting off — a hold-down demand at that node.

For each ULS combination, every node showing negative FY contributes to the overturning moment about the pivot line:
Moverturn = Σ (|FY,i| × di)
where di is the perpendicular distance from node i to the pivot line (m).

The minimum counterweight required to stabilise the moment at that analysis line:
CWmin,line = Moverturn ÷ (γstb × LCW)

This is evaluated at each distinct row of base nodes (analysis line) independently for every ULS combination. The line-by-line CWmin values within a given combination are summed to obtain the total counterweight demand for that combination — each analysis line represents a separate strip of the scaffold base, and summing across all strips gives the total counterweight required to stabilise the structure as a whole. The combination producing the largest total demand is the governing case; the minimum design counterweight equals that total. Results for the governing combination are tabulated below.

F.6 — Residual Hold-down Demand at Backspan Base Nodes
EQU partial factor effect Under ULS EQU combinations, the destabilising factor (1.5) exceeds the stabilising CW factor ({{ counterweight.governing_cw_factor }}).
